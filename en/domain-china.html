Field guide · China availability

China availability: can you use it, what does it cost, where does data live?

“Supports China” is not one line. You need six columns: can it be activated, in which region, how it is priced, where data stays, what the licence allows, and who issues the contract and invoice. Every cell needs an official entry point and a check date. Write “unknown” for blanks; do not fill them with “probably fine”.

For Anyone choosing, buying or deploying AI in China You leave with A six-column availability matrix and a three-item procurement order Time 15 minutes to read; 1–2 hours per vendor Last checked 25 Nov 2026

This is a procurement and verification framework, not a legal, compliance or pricing conclusion. Vendor availability, prices, contracts and data terms remain with official documents, signed contracts and on-site verification.

If you only have five minutes
  1. Fill six columns separately: availability, price, residency, licence, procurement and support.
  2. A vendor landing page is a lead, not a conclusion.
  3. Write “unknown” for blanks; do not make promises for the vendor.

The full guide starts below. Use the tables for reference; you do not need to memorise them.

One concrete job

Take one concrete job: in Shanghai, choose a model API for a 20-person team, with a Chinese invoice, domestic data residency and token pricing. Fill the six columns, then compare three vendors.

Write the six columns into a verifiable matrix before comparing models. “China” under one vendor is not the same contract, the same region or the same price.

1 · Six columns, not one “available in China”

Split “available in China” into six columns so every answer can be checked. Availability asks whether an account can be opened and the model is actually offered. Price asks token, hour, seat or minimum spend. Residency asks where training, inference, logs, backups and support access sit. Licence asks what is permitted for model, weights, output and redistribution. Procurement asks contract entity, invoice, reseller, acceptance and SLA. Support asks Chinese tickets, incident response and version updates.

The six columns cannot substitute for one another. A model being offered does not mean the contract permits your use. Data staying in-country does not mean logs or backups do. A low price does not include support or refunds. An invoice does not mean the contracting entity carries data responsibility. Before purchase, mark each column “evidence / unknown / not applicable”, not just “supported”.

2 · Rows by delivery form: cloud API, dedicated, private, local weights, open source, integrator

One vendor may sell cloud API, dedicated instances, private software and appliances at the same time. Their data paths, prices and contracts differ. Rows by delivery form prevent “cloud API is available” from becoming “private deployment works too”.

An open repository is another form: public code or a licence does not mean commercial weights or a hosted service. Separate “can download” from “can run in production”, and “community edition” from “enterprise edition”.

4 · Residency: training, inference, logs, backups and support access

“Data stays in-country” often means only where inference requests are stored. It may exclude logs, backups, monitoring, ticket attachments and cross-border support access. Draw five flows: training data, inference input, output, logs and backups, support/operations access. For each ask where it sits, who can access it, how long it is kept and how it is deleted.

The contract needs specific regions, subprocessors and retention periods. “Complies with local law” is not enough; “encrypted” is not enough either, because encryption does not change location or who can access it. If support is handled abroad, write separate approval, redaction and audit for support access.

Training data
Ask: Used for training, where, can opt out
Common gap: “No training” only on a marketing page

Inference input
Ask: Where request/context sit, how long
Common gap: Storage region but not logs

Output
Ask: Cached, used for evaluation or improvement
Common gap: Output terms separate from privacy policy

Logs/backups
Ask: Region, retention, deletion, access
Common gap: Offshore backups undisclosed

Support access
Ask: Cross-border, approval, redaction, audit
Common gap: Ticket attachments carry customer data

5 · Price: tokens, hours, seats and minimum spend

The unit price on a price page is not the bill. Add minimum spend, committed volume, egress, storage, logs, support, tax, reseller fees and retries. Use “per accepted task” or “per finished minute” as the denominator so the number is comparable with in-house, outsourcing or another vendor.

Split price into three parts: fixed (minimum/platform/support), usage (tokens/hours/seats/storage), and variable (egress/retries/human review). Comparing only the usage unit often misses fixed and variable costs; comparing only the first-month discount often misses renewal and exit costs.

Fixed
Ask: Minimum spend, platform fee, support fee
Why: Most often missed at low volume

Usage
Ask: Tokens, hours, seats, storage
Why: Different units are not comparable

Variable
Ask: Egress, retries, logs, human review
Why: Main source of production bill variance

Renewal/exit
Ask: Price increase, minimum term, export, deletion
Why: Real cost of switching

6 · Licence and contract: model, weights, output and redistribution

Model service terms, weights licence, output terms and redistribution terms are four different documents. A cloud API usually grants service use, not weights. Local weights may allow commercial use but restrict redistribution. Output terms may allow you to use generated content but not to claim the model is yours.

The procurement contract should state who may use it, for what, at what volume, for how long, whether redistribution is allowed and what happens after termination. An open-source licence does not replace a contract, and a contract does not replace a licence. If they conflict, stop and get written clarification from legal and the vendor.

Model service
Typical question: Commercial API use, restricted uses
Evidence: Terms, order

Weights
Typical question: Download, commercial use, fine-tune, redistribute
Evidence: Weights licence text

Output
Typical question: Ownership, brand/ad use
Evidence: Output terms, platform rules

Redistribution
Typical question: Embed in a product, sell to third parties
Evidence: Contract, licence, written clarification

7 · Evidence levels and check dates

The same “supported” carries different weight depending on where it comes from. Official product pages and docs are entry points; quotes and contracts are commitments; sales emails and meeting notes are leads; blogs, community posts and third-party rankings are not contract evidence. Each cell records source type, link or contract ID, check date and owner.

Treat “unknown” as a formal state, not a todo. An unknown cell cannot enter a production decision: get evidence, mark it not applicable, or reduce scope. Recheck the matrix quarterly; prices, regions, model catalogues and terms can change.

  • Level A: contract, order, DPA and SLA text with signing entity and date.
  • Level B: official docs, console screenshots and announcements with link and check date.
  • Level C: sales email, quote, meeting notes; leads only.
  • Level D: blogs, communities, rankings, rumours; not contract evidence.

8 · Failure branches: “supported” that does not work

The most common China procurement failure is not model quality but mismatched evidence: the website says supported, the contract does not cover it; the region says China, the model is not offered; private deployment still calls an external API; the price is from an international site, the invoice from a reseller; data stays in-country, support access is abroad. Trace the symptom back to the matrix, not just to sales confirmation.

Website says yes, contract says no
Suspect first: Marketing page vs contract schedule
Check: Put use, region and volume into the contract

China site, model absent
Suspect first: Site region vs model catalogue
Check: Open it in the console; keep a screenshot

“Private” still egresses
Suspect first: Updates, telemetry, support channel
Check: Packet capture/firewall test; put in acceptance

Low price, high bill
Suspect first: Minimum, egress, retries, tax
Check: Three-month total cost

Invoice from wrong entity
Suspect first: Reseller, contract entity
Check: Check signing entity and data responsibility

Data local, support abroad
Suspect first: Cross-border support, ticket attachments
Check: Require redaction, approval and audit

Commercial use, no redistribution
Suspect first: Model/weights/output terms conflated
Check: Read each text; get written clarification

SLA without remedy
Suspect first: Availability commitment and credits
Check: Read remedy and termination clauses

Rule of thumb: if a cell says “supported” without a link, contract clause or check date, treat it as unknown and keep it out of the production decision.

9 · A minimum path to a verified matrix

Start with one real task and one data class, not the whole company. Run one procurement cycle through the matrix before widening the vendor set.

  • Define task and data class: use, sensitivity, volume, cost of error.
  • Rows by delivery form: cloud API, dedicated, private, local weights, open source, integrator.
  • Keep 2–3 candidates per row; open official entries before third-party rankings.
  • Fill six columns: availability, price, residency, licence, procurement, support.
  • Ask the 18 procurement questions; answers need links or clauses.
  • Run a minimum trial: real data, real account, real bill.
  • Have legal/security/finance each own a column; do not leave it to procurement alone.
  • Put unknowns into the risk list with owner and deadline.
  • After signing, recheck price, region, catalogue and terms quarterly.
Done means six columns have evidence, unknowns have owners, the trial bill is recomputable and the contract covers your use — not that a big vendor was chosen.
