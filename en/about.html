About Specul

A network of sites that connects learning AI, choosing tools, reading models, browsing papers and trying real work. Not a media outlet, not a leaderboard, and not a course sales page.

What we are trying to solve

There is no shortage of AI information; what is scarce is information that lands on a specific decision. People trying to use AI usually get stuck on four questions: what is this, which one should I use, can I trust it, and who do I ask when it breaks. The ten Specul sites answer those four questions and connect them with one standard for sources, dates and limits.

So we do not publish fifty news flashes a day, and we do not rank the “strongest model”. We build archives you can check: every judgement carries an official source where possible, a verification date, and a clear note on what it does not claim.

Three things we believe

Limits before conclusions. Every product, model and paper says who it suits, who it does not, and what it has not shown. A recommendation without limits is an advertisement.

Sources matter more than opinions. If it can be linked to official docs, a spec, a paper or a repository, we link it. If not, it is marked “to verify”. We would rather leave a blank than invent a plausible number.

Dates are part of the content. AI products turn over every few months. Every profile carries a verification date. Out-of-date entries are marked, not silently rewritten, so you can judge for yourself.

What we do not do

We do not sell rankings, publish a “strongest model” list, sell courses, turn unverified numbers into specs, turn “publicly downloadable” into “commercially usable”, or treat model output as fact. We do not give investment, legal or medical advice.
